# Nota — Market and AI Strategy Research

## Decision summary

Nota should remain a three-phase company:

1. **Phase 1 — Québec City:** prove a deadline-priced marketplace for basic,
   repeatable financing and refinancing acts.
2. **Phase 2 — Québec and Canada:** add the act-specific operating layer,
   expand province-wide, and enter British Columbia through its real-property
   notary lane. Treat Ontario and the other provinces as lawyer, commissioner,
   or regulated-provider markets rather than assuming Québec's notary category
   travels unchanged.
3. **Phase 3 — global protocol:** run Chamber-grade online-act pilots, then
   enter selected civil-law countries through local notarial institutions and
   approved identity/signing rails. Only after notarial proof should Nota package
   the hidden-capacity protocol for other urgent services.

The strategic asset is not a generic AI assistant. It is a market protocol that
turns a hard deadline into qualified demand while providers keep their private
calendar hidden. AI increases the value of each retained request by preparing a
reviewable, act-specific dossier; it does not replace the notary's professional
judgment.

## What an investor-grade AI plan must prove

Current investor-readiness guidance emphasizes a coherent investment thesis,
verifiable traction, a financial model connected to the operating plan, unit
economics, governance, and scenario analysis. The UK government's investor
readiness framework specifically calls out contracts or paid pilots, retention
and engagement, pipeline dates and values, realistic forecasts, unit economics,
governance, and best/base/worst-case scenarios ([UK Investor Readiness
Essentials](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unlocking-space-for-investment-growth-hub/track-1-investor-readiness-essentials);
[UK Financial Model Essentials](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unlocking-space-for-investment-growth-hub/track-1-financial-model-essentials)).

AI changes what has to be added to that standard plan. Nota's plan should carry
an evidence ledger for every AI claim:

| AI-plan requirement | Nota evidence to build |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome baseline | Minutes of preparation time, number of manual touches, missing-document rate, rework rate, time-to-ready dossier, and completion-on-date before Nota AI. |
| Act-specific evaluation | A versioned test set for each act; factual accuracy, required-field recall, citation/provenance, refusal/escalation rate, and notary acceptance rate. No model goes live on a new act without a named reviewer and a release gate. |
| Human accountability | The notary validates every output; AI cannot sign, provide the legal opinion, or silently change a retained offer. Every generated field carries provenance and an audit event. |
| Data rights and privacy | Purpose limitation, consent and retention rules, training-data lineage, tenant isolation, deletion workflow, Canadian/European hosting strategy, and a written rule that client documents are not used to train a general model by default. |
| Model economics | Cost per completed act, tokens or inference per dossier, latency, fallback-model cost, third-party API dependency, and gross contribution after payment processing and support. |
| Adoption | Time-to-first-dossier, weekly active notary offices, repeat acts per office, percentage of outputs accepted without edits, and retained-act conversion. |
| Safety and monitoring | Incident taxonomy, red-team cases, drift monitoring, rollback, human override, and periodic review. NIST's AI RMF organizes this as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage and stresses validity, reliability, safety, security, accountability, transparency, explainability, privacy, and fairness ([NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook); [NIST Trustworthy AI](https://www.nist.gov/trustworthy-and-responsible-ai)). |

The phrase “save 99% of effort” should remain a North Star, not a present-tense
claim. The investable claim is: **each phase will publish a baseline, a target,
an evaluation set, and a go/no-go threshold.** Microsoft guidance for AI-agent
business plans similarly recommends setting simple success metrics before
development, using them as investment gates, and measuring after deployment
([Microsoft AI-agent business strategy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ai-agents/business-strategy-plan)).

## Canada: province-by-province entry logic

The core Canadian question is not population alone. It is whether the local
professional can legally and economically perform a repeatable act, whether a
deadline creates urgency, whether digital preparation is acceptable, and whether
Nota can reach a recognized professional body.

| Market | Legal/service fit | Opportunity | Recommended sequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Québec** | Direct fit: civil-law notaries, mortgage and real-property acts, a mature technological-act framework. | Current SAM: approximately 110,000 financing/refinancing acts and about 215 M$ of professional-fee value; 90,369 Québec residential sales in 2024. | **Phase 1–2.** Québec City first; then Montréal, Gatineau, Sherbrooke, Trois-Rivières, Saguenay, Lévis and the rest of Québec. |
| **British Columbia** | Strongest non-Québec fit: BC notaries routinely handle residential conveyancing and mortgage matters, alongside lawyers ([BC Notaries — services](https://bcnotary.com/services/)). | A natural extension of the financing, refinancing, purchase and sale workflow, with high-value property transactions and distance clients. | **Phase 2.** Enter through a provincial notary partner and a narrow real-property catalogue. |
| **Ontario** | Notary role is materially narrower: a notary can witness oaths and certify copies; lawyers and paralegals are commissioners, and Ontario states that notaries cannot notarize remotely ([Ontario notary guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/information-notaries-public-ontario)). | Large legal, mortgage, affidavit, commissioning and closing markets, but not a Québec-style notarial-act market. | **Phase 2 or 3.** Repackage the protocol for lawyers, commissioners, title/closing providers, and urgent document services. |
| **Alberta** | No Québec-equivalent civil-law notary lane; legal and real-property work is lawyer-led. | Urgent mortgage, closing, land, corporate and commissioner workflows; strong housing and population-growth signals. | **Phase 2–3.** Validate through a law-firm or closing-provider channel, not a notary marketplace assumption. |
| **Saskatchewan / Manitoba** | Treat as lawyer/commissioner-led markets unless a specific regulated act proves otherwise. | Smaller but useful regional tests for remote access, rural capacity and urgent document workflows. | **Phase 2–3.** Enter after the Québec operating layer is repeatable. |
| **New Brunswick / Nova Scotia / PEI / Newfoundland and Labrador** | Lawyer, commissioner and provincial-regulator structures differ from Québec. | Regional access and distance create a strong use case; demand is lower-density and partnership-led. | **Phase 3.** Test one Atlantic corridor with a professional-body or lender partner. |
| **Yukon / Northwest Territories / Nunavut** | Access and distance are the primary problem; local rules and capacity are highly specific. | Small-volume, high-value access-to-justice and remote-service pilots. | **Phase 3.** Only with government, legal-aid, or local professional sponsorship. |

Canada is a good first export market because the customer language, payments,
privacy expectations and mortgage channels are familiar. It is not one legal
market. The product should therefore export the **accept / counter-propose /
request information / pass** protocol and the dossier infrastructure, not a
single definition of “notary.”

## International market screen

UINL reports 93 member notariats, including 22 of the 27 EU member countries
and 15 of the 19 G20 countries; it says civil law is present in almost 120
countries, representing roughly two-thirds of the world's population and more
than 60% of world GDP ([UINL — About us](https://uinl.org/mission/about-us/)).
That is a credible expansion surface, but it is not one product market. Each
country needs its own act catalogue, professional rules, identity standard,
signing provider, data policy, language, pricing, and regulator relationship.

### Country launch scorecard

Before entering a country, score 1–5 on: (1) repeatable act volume, (2) hard
deadlines, (3) hidden provider capacity, (4) digital identity/signing readiness,
(5) regulator or professional-body access, and (6) ability to operate with a
local partner. A country enters the base plan only with an average of 4 or
better, a local sponsor, and a paid pilot. The score is an internal strategic
inference, not a published market statistic.

## Other industries: only after notarial proof

The market primitive is broader than notarial work, but the compliance burden
changes by vertical. The best adjacent categories share four properties: a hard
customer deadline, fragmented providers, private or variable capacity, and a
service that can be qualified before human fulfillment.

| Vertical | Why it fits | Main risk | Entry condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency home repair | Immediate need, variable technician capacity, simple accept/propose/pass behavior. | Safety, quality, insurance and dispatch liability. | Local licensed-provider network and category-specific triage. |
| Field technicians and maintenance | B2B deadlines, geographic matching, repeat demand, provider calendars rarely public. | SLA liability and parts availability. | Anchor enterprise customer with measurable response-time pain. |
| Legal, translation and document services | Remote delivery, deadline-heavy, AI can structure intake and context. | Unauthorized practice, confidentiality, language accuracy. | Professional review and no autonomous legal conclusion. |
| Inspections, appraisals and compliance visits | Transaction deadlines and regional capacity scarcity. | Licensing, conflicts and evidentiary quality. | Verified credential graph and chain-of-custody controls. |
| Logistics and specialist courier | The deadline is the product; supply is dynamic. | Insurance, tracking and operational complexity. | Narrow high-value lane before general dispatch. |
| Healthcare, childcare and safety-critical work | Very large need and urgent demand. | High harm if matching or AI fails. | Do not enter until governance, insurance and sector partners exist. |

## Three phases with gates

### Phase 1 — Québec City: prove the market

**Customer:** financing and refinancing requests with a required date, amount,
language, location and document context.

**Notary:** free to join and browse; no agenda sharing; accept, counter-propose,
request information or pass; confirmed act can sync to the notary's calendar.

**Business model:** the notary keeps 100% of professional fees. Nota charges its
own published service line and date-priority line only on completion. This keeps
the normal act value visible, lets a scarce date command a premium, and makes the
three-sided value exchange legible.

**Gate:** 25–30 active Québec City notaries, 25+ qualified bids/month by month
6, fill rate above 45% by month 6 and above 60% by month 12, 244 completed acts
in the base case, and a measured urgency curve showing whether clients actually
pay for time.

### Phase 2 — Québec and Canada: build the operating layer

**AI product:** documents, answers, preferences, deadline and act type become a
structured context for a model evaluated specifically for that act. The model
prepares missing-document requests, checklists, draft inputs and integration
payloads; the notary validates every output.

**Geography:** first all Québec, then British Columbia's real-property notary
lane. Ontario and the other provinces are separate product packages for lawyers,
commissioners, title/closing providers or other regulated professionals.

**Gate:** measured preparation-time reduction, notary acceptance rate, zero
unreviewed legal outputs, repeat usage by offices, a positive contribution
margin per retained act after model/infrastructure/support costs, and a signed
partner path in each new jurisdiction.

### Phase 3 — Chamber-grade online acts and global protocol

The institutional product includes secure live video, identity and capacity
checks, confidentiality checks, informed consent, electronic signature, evidence
logs, certified-copy delivery and a complete audit trail. In Québec, the pilot
must be designed with the Chambre, privacy counsel, approved signing providers
and selected notaries; the current legal position on remote acts remains a
constraint to respect, not an assumption to ignore ([CNQ remote technological
act](https://www.cnq.org/en/your-notary/a-digital-professional/signing-a-technology-based-notarial-act-remotely/);
[CNQ technology providers](https://www.cnq.org/fournisseurs-de-solutions-technologiques-aux-notaires/)).

The global sequence should be **France / Italy / Spain / Netherlands / Belgium**
for the first European corridors, **Brazil / Colombia** for the first Latin
American corridors, and then a scorecard-led rollout across the remaining UINL
members. Each launch requires a local professional-body relationship, an
approved identity/signing rail, local privacy and data-retention design, a
bounded act catalogue, and a paid pilot. The protocol can then expand into
other urgent services only when the notarial system has proven trust, safety and
unit economics.

## Investor diligence dashboard

The monthly dashboard should make the business legible in one page:

| Lens | Leading indicators | Investor proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquidity | Qualified bids, active notaries, fill rate, time-to-first-response, date-on-time rate. | The market clears. |
| Customer value | Quote conversion, urgency-premium acceptance, abandonment, repeat/referral rate. | Customers pay for the outcome and the date. |
| Notary value | Retained acts per office, incremental income, preparation minutes saved, output acceptance rate. | Supply earns more and works less. |
| AI quality | Act-specific accuracy, missing-field recall, edit rate, escalation rate, incidents. | AI is controlled, not magical. |
| Economics | Nota revenue/act, payment cost, AI cost/act, support cost/act, contribution margin, CAC payback. | Growth compounds rather than amplifies losses. |
| Defensibility | Proprietary price × deadline × act dataset, integrations, partners, compliance evidence. | The moat is operational and data-backed. |
| Capital efficiency | Burn multiple, runway, milestone spend, base/upside/downside scenario. | The ask buys a measurable inflection point. |

## Research limitations

Published data is uneven. Québec does not publish a clean province-wide average
wait time for a notarial appointment, and several countries publish act counts
without publishing the share that is urgent, online, or capacity-constrained.
Those gaps are not reasons to invent precision. Phase 1 should instrument the
missing data directly: request-to-first-response, request-to-acceptance,
acceptance-to-signature, on-time completion, realized date premium, and provider
incremental income. Those are the proprietary numbers that convert this research
from a market story into an investable proof.
